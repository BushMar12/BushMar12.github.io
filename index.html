VoiceEye

On-device vision assistant for people with low vision

Status
Deployed PWA, live on Cloudflare Pages
Role
Sole developer: data, model, MLOps, app
Period
April to May 2026
Evidence
Public repository, 144 unit tests
Source
github.com/BushMar12/VoiceEye ↗
voiceeye.pages.dev ↗
Stack
PyTorch, Ultralytics YOLO26, Optuna, ClearML, ONNX Runtime Web, TypeScript, React, Vitest, GitHub Actions, Cloudflare Workers AI

VoiceEye reads a phone's camera feed and tells a low-vision pedestrian what matters nearby: a car pulling in, a person close ahead. Detection runs inside the mobile browser, so camera frames never leave the device.

Problem

The detector had to fit a phone browser's memory and battery budget. The harder part was speech. A crowded street produces dozens of detections per second, and reading all of them aloud is useless. The app had to choose what to say and when.

Review note

The 10 FPS figure is the app's throttle target for battery life. Detection runs every 100 ms by design.

Decision record

  1. D1A

    Merged COCO 2017, Open Images v7 and Mapillary Vistas into one 48-class set, adding six safety-critical classes COCO lacks.

    WhyNo single public dataset covered street hazards for blind navigation. Automated validation and stratified splits keep each rebuild reproducible, and ClearML registers the 35 GB of images by reference, so nothing is re-uploaded.

  2. D2B · 2

    Chose YOLO26n and tuned the model with Optuna Bayesian search inside a ClearML pipeline.

    WhyThe nano model fits the phone budget. HPO trials run on a separate GPU queue so the pipeline controller never blocks the only GPU worker.

  3. D31 · 2

    Ran inference in the browser with ONNX Runtime Web and fixed Safari memory crashes with reused buffers and explicit tensor disposal.

    WhyOn-device inference keeps camera frames private and removes network latency. The runtime bundle dropped from 24 MB to about 12 MB.

  4. D43 · 4

    Tracked objects across frames with IoU matching and EMA-smoothed velocity, then estimated distance with a pinhole camera model.

    WhyTrack IDs stop the app repeating itself, and a growing box marks an object moving toward the user. Box area sets the zone: safe below 25% of the frame, danger above 50%.

  5. D55 · 6

    Capped speech at 1, 3 or 6 announcements per 2-second window, with a hazard override for vehicles jumping into the danger zone.

    WhyUsers need the bus pulling in, not every chair. Same-class objects close together are grouped into one phrase, such as "3 people, ~4m".

  6. D67 · 8

    Added a slower, on-demand lane: a vision-language model for "describe", "read" and "find" voice commands.

    WhyDetection names objects but cannot read a sign. Qwen3-VL runs through Ollama in development, and Cloudflare Workers AI serves production without a server to maintain.

  7. D7B · C

    Gated every release on mAP@50 ≥ 0.40, then let GitHub Actions pull the ONNX file and open a pull request.

    WhyA model reaches users only through a reviewed pull request, never through a manual file copy.

Lesson

Phone memory was the first blocker, ahead of accuracy. A good detector still overwhelms the user without rules for what to say.

JetRacer RL

A camera-only robot car, trained in simulation and driven on a real track

Status
Lab-tested on the real car, 20 Oct 2026
Role
Main code contributor in a UTS team. Owned the obstacle stop, simulator pipeline and car runtime.
Credit
Steering policy and line-repaint method: team
Evidence
Lab log, held-out evaluation, offline replay
Source
Private team repository. Code walkthrough on request.
Stack
PyTorch, Stable-Baselines3 (PPO, SAC, DQN), gym-donkeycar, Isaac Sim, ONNX Runtime, Jetson Nano, AWS SageMaker Studio, uv, pytest

The car sees the track through one fisheye camera and runs on a Jetson Nano. Policies train in the Donkey Car simulator. My part made the car stop for an object in its lane and drive on once someone removed the object.

Problem

Policies trained to drive well in simulation broke on the real car. The real camera had a 160° fisheye lens, pink edge shading and paler track lines. The real steering turned at about 75% of the simulated car's. The first obstacle-stop policy passed every simulator test and then stopped on every real straight.

Review note · limits

Objects beside the lane caused 21 stops. The clear road caused 11 false stops, 4 of them from folds in the track mat. The test was two drives, about 6 minutes, labelled by eye.

Decision record

  1. D1S1 · 2

    Matched the simulator to the real camera: measured mount height and tilt, modelled the fisheye lens and normalised colour on both sides.

    WhyWhite balance alone cannot fix a camera whose colour shifts across the frame, so shading, creases, glare and steering response are randomised in training.

  2. D2S1

    Mirrored half of all training episodes.

    WhyThe simulator drives the mat only counter-clockwise. Mirrored frames with negated steering teach the clockwise lap on a symmetric track.

  3. D3S2

    Rendered obstacle approach clips in Isaac Sim, from 1.6 m to contact in 1 cm steps, and pasted the matching frame into the simulated camera each tick.

    WhyThe Donkey simulator has no realistic objects. Clips matched to the car's 17° camera tilt put real-looking obstacles in the lane at the right scale.

  4. D4T · 3 · E

    Switched the STOP/GO learner from PPO to DQN, trained beside the frozen steering policy, and exported both as one ONNX model.

    WhyThe PPO version never stopped deterministically. One model with outputs [steering, go] keeps the car's runtime simple: the car stops when go < 0.

  5. D5S3

    Pasted real lab backgrounds into the far band of training frames, first in half of the episodes (v2), then in every episode (v3).

    WhyVersion 1 read "GO" from the simulator's far track edge, a cue the real lab never shows. Removing the cue removed the shortcut.

  6. D6S4 · R

    Selected checkpoints on held-out real lab frames, scored by GO and STOP rates and AUROC.

    WhySimulator evaluations hid the shortcut, and v3 even looked worse in simulation as training went on. Real frames told the truth.

  7. D74

    Stopped every drive on its time limit, any error, a stalled camera or a control step over 150 ms.

    WhyA robot car on a lab floor needs a hard stop independent of the browser connection.

Table 02.1 · Lab test, 20 Oct 2026, version 3 on the car
SituationWhat the car did

Lesson

A simulator score is a weak proxy for the world. I now hold out real data for model selection from day one and log every real run for replay.

Photo or video in, itemised repair quote in Australian dollars out

Status

Stack
PyTorch, Ultralytics, RF-DETR, RT-DETR, Faster R-CNN, ClearML, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, SQLAlchemy, Docker, Ollama, Qwen 2.5, Flutter

Insurance claims and repair quotes start with a person reviewing photos. This system finds six damage types, prices the repair with rules a customer is able to check, and answers questions about the result.

Problem

The most accurate detector was not the right one to serve. The system needed a model small and fast enough for a web API, a pricing method a person is able to audit, and an assistant running without paid APIs.

Decision record

  1. D1A

    Tripled the CarDD training set from 1,971 to 5,913 images with augmentation, standardised at 640×640.

    WhySix classes from under two thousand images left rare damage types thin.

  2. D2B

    Benchmarked six detectors under one protocol: YOLO variants, RT-DETR, RF-DETR and Faster R-CNN.

    WhyOne-stage, transformer and two-stage designs trade accuracy, size and latency differently. The choice needed numbers, not habit.

  3. D3B · 3

    Served YOLO26m instead of the top-scoring RF-DETR-m.

    WhyA 42 MB model at about 23 ms per image suits a web API. The cost was 4.34 points of mAP@0.5 (Table 03.1).

  4. D44

    Priced repairs with explicit rules: base cost per damage type × severity (1×, 2× or 3.5×) + labour at $120 per hour + 10% GST.

    WhyEvery line of a quote traces back to a stated rule.

  5. D5C

    Tracked dataset versions, experiments, model promotion and inference monitoring in ClearML.

    WhySix architectures produce many runs, and the registry records which weights the API serves.

  6. D65 · 6

    Ran Qwen 2.5 locally through Ollama, with the saved assessment as context, and stored reports and chats in PostgreSQL.

    WhyThe assistant answers about this car's damage, not cars in general, and costs nothing per call.

What-If Flight Replay

Teaching a quadrotor to survive a failed rotor, tested once on 2,772 held-out missions

Status
Pre-registered study complete. Paper in draft.
Role
Built the Isaac Sim and PX4 track of a team study, and wrote its report chapter.
Credit
Rewind method and the MuJoCo study: team
Context
UTS 32933 Research Project, Spring 2026. PX4 study: 6 to 11 October 2026.
Evidence
Analysis plan hashed and timestamped before the held-out run. Run records for every result.
Source
Private team repository. Walkthrough on request.
Stack
Isaac Sim 5.1, Isaac Lab, Pegasus Simulator, PX4 v1.14.3 (SITL, MAVLink offboard), Stable-Baselines3 PPO, PyTorch, MuJoCo, Qwen3-VL, ClearML, pytest

My Master's research team studies why a small AI drone pilot crashes, by rewinding each crash in simulation and replaying it with one thing changed. My track took the recovery question down to the rotors: a learned policy takes over from the PX4 autopilot when a rotor fails, then holds position, continues the mission or lands.

Problem

PX4 keeps flying only while the damaged rotor still carries its share of the hover thrust. In a 180-flight sweep, every flight above that margin recovered and every flight below it crashed. Past that point the drone needs a controller trained on failure. The test also had to be one I had no way to tune after seeing the results.

Earlier exploratory work on the same track flew zero-shot vision-language pilots (Qwen3-VL, AeroVLA) over the recovery layer and moved the team's MuJoCo-trained pilots into Isaac Sim.

Review note · limits

Simulation only, no hardware. The takeover fires on the injected fault itself, so no fault detector was tested. The policy reads the simulator's true state, with no wind or sensor noise in this study. One rotor fails at a time. Safe rates range from 68% to 88% across training seeds.

Decision record

  1. D1A

    Rebuilt the study's procedural city in Isaac Sim from the same seeds.

    WhyThe team's MuJoCo world gives exact rewind but flies a simplified drone. Isaac Sim models each rotor, and matching scenes keep the two studies comparable.

  2. D2B · 5

    Never told the policy which rotor failed. Its input stacks recent frames, including its own last action.

    WhyA real drone gets no fault label. The policy has to infer the failure from how the drone responds to its own commands.

  3. D3B · C

    Trained on moving references (hold, travel, land), with a second family trained only to hold as the comparison.

    WhyThe takeover happens mid-flight at cruise speed. Hold-only training never sees that state, and the registered test measured the cost (H2, Table 04.2).

  4. D42 · 4

    Left mild faults to PX4 and took over only below a measured thrust margin.

    WhyPX4 alone completed all 90 mild cases. Taking control away there adds risk for no gain.

  5. D5D

    Calibrated each seed's speed limit and landing threshold on validation scenes, then froze them.

    WhyEvery tuning choice was made on scenes the final test never used.

  6. D6D · 6

    Wrote the hypotheses and analysis down first, timestamped the document's hash, and flew the held-out scenes once.

    WhyA plan fixed before the data exists leaves no room to pick the flattering analysis. One of the four hypotheses failed and is reported as such.

  7. D73 · 6

    Gave every mission exactly one outcome and kept infrastructure errors in the denominator.

    WhyDropped runs inflate a success rate. The held-out run finished with 0 errors and 0 missing cases.

05.1

Multimodal Stock Return Prediction

A 1.67M-parameter two-stream model for next-day TSLA returns. A Transformer encodes 60 days of price and technical indicators, a multi-kernel 1D CNN encodes Alpha Vantage news sentiment, and cross-modal attention joins them. Splits are chronological, and scalers are fitted on training data only.

The evaluation compares the fusion model with LSTM, GRU, Transformer and XGBoost baselines, plus a 3-configuration × 5-seed ablation. The data loader caches Alpha Vantage results and resumes after rate limits. SHAP analysis and a 7-tab Streamlit dashboard. Final results are still being run.

The projects on these sheets share a pattern. Most leave the notebook and end on a device or behind an API, with data pipelines, tests and release gates around the model. The research project ends in a test planned before the results existed. I am looking for a Sydney team shipping machine learning, AI or data systems into real use.
